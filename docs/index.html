Math and units, offline

Arithmetic, trig, powers, percentages and unit conversion, evaluated on the device. No network needed.

Two models, one sentence

A free open model answers first. A second joins for dates, numbers and fresh facts, and a third breaks a tie. When nothing agrees, you see both answers. Every answer names its source.

Graphs

Type y = x^2 or plot sin(x) and get the curve, sampled on device.

Time-sensitive answers say when they were checked, so you know how fresh they are.

Works offline.

Math is always on the device. Download the offline pack once and Wikipedia questions answer with no signal too: the 9,000 most-read English articles, about 1.9 MB. Nothing downloads on its own.

Each answer from the pack names its article and the date the pack was built. When a question is not in the pack, it says so instead of guessing. iPhone, Mac, Android and the web all have it.

Measured.

A 154-question exam against the live service: Groq's free models score 152, and our own small model, running on a Mac mini, scores 147. A second exam of 1,049 questions runs every week.

Math and units are checked offline, 810 of 810. Method and every number are in the benchmark.

Open source, one dollar

Nimble is open source under Apache 2.0. A ground-up rewrite of the original Maybulb/Nimble in native SwiftUI. The web app is free. The iPhone and Mac apps are $0.99 once, which pays for signed builds and the answer service. No API keys, no accounts, no subscriptions.
