Privacy Policy

Nimble Answers · Last updated 12 October 2026

Nimble Answers has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics and no advertising. Nothing about you is collected, stored or sold. The app is not able to identify you, because it never asks who you are.

Three ways to answer

You pick how far a question travels. On the web, AI answers are on by default and Private mode keeps every question on the device. The iPhone, Mac and Apple Watch apps ask once, the first time they open, and keep an AI Answers switch in Settings.

  • Free cloud (the default). Your question goes to the app's own answering service at nimble-answers.trommatic.workers.dev, which asks open models (OpenAI's gpt-oss and Alibaba's Qwen on Groq, with Alibaba's Qwen and Google's Gemma on Cloudflare Workers AI as backup), and to DuckDuckGo and Wikipedia for sources. Groq and Cloudflare each see the question text and nothing else.
  • Your own key. Pick Claude or OpenAI in Settings and enter your key, and the question goes straight from your device to Anthropic or OpenAI. Pick Ollama and it goes to the Ollama server you run. The key stays on your device.
  • Private. Turn on Private mode in Settings and nothing leaves your device. Math, units, graphs and the offline Wikipedia pack still answer; on an iPhone or Mac with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model answers too. Anything else says plainly that it cannot be answered while Private is on.

What leaves your device

Only the text of your question, and only when answering it needs something the app does not already have.

  • Math, units and graphs never leave. They are worked out on your device.
  • Live cards ask the source directly. Weather asks Open-Meteo, currency asks Frankfurter or ExchangeRate-API, prices ask CoinGecko or Stooq, scores ask ESPN, and news reads public feeds from outlets such as the BBC, CBC, NPR and The Guardian. A word definition asks Wiktionary or the Free Dictionary.
  • Asking about a website sends that site's address to the answering service, which reads the page and answers from it.
  • The microphone uses your device's own speech recognition. On iPhone and Mac it runs on the device where Apple supports it. In a browser it is the browser's own service; in Chrome that service is Google's.
  • Listen reads answers aloud with your device's own voice. Nothing is sent.

These requests carry your question and nothing else. No identifier, no account, no device fingerprint, no location, no contacts, no history of what you asked before.

What is kept

The answering service keeps no log of who asked what. Three things live there briefly, and none is linked to a person:

  • A cached answer for a question, keyed by the question's text alone, kept for up to six hours (one hour for anything time-sensitive) so a repeat question costs nothing.
  • A daily count of how many AI calls and cache hits it served, as numbers only, so the free AI lasts the day.
  • A short-lived count of requests per network address, purely to enforce a rate limit. It expires on its own.

Your recent searches, their answers and your settings are stored on your device. On iPhone and Mac, recent searches also sync between your own devices through your iCloud account. Clear them in History, or delete the app to remove them.

Third parties

Each service named above receives the question text when a request reaches it, and handles that request under its own privacy policy. The app shares nothing with them beyond the question itself.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the revised version will be posted on this page with a new date above.
